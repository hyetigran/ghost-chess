# King capture replaces checkmate/stalemate as the game-ending condition

[ADR-0008](./0008-attack-based-fog-of-war-vision.md) means a player can no longer always tell whether their own king is under attack — that information is exactly what vision now gates, the same as any other piece of board state. Standard chess's check/checkmate rules assume the opposite: that both players always know, with certainty, whether the king to move is in check, and that a move leaving your own king in check is simply illegal. Keeping that assumption under partial visibility would mean either quietly giving players true-board information their vision doesn't entitle them to (defeating ADR-0008's whole point), or enforcing a legality rule players can't reason about from what they're shown, which is worse than not having the rule at all.

We decided to drop check and checkmate entirely. A move that leaves the mover's own king capturable next turn is legal, not prevented — the game only ends when a king is actually captured. `result` gains `king_captured` (mover wins) in place of `checkmate`/`stalemate`; `draw` now also covers the rare case of the side to move having zero legal moves at all (stalemate's replacement — genuinely rare once check-safety no longer restricts anything, but not impossible), alongside the unchanged threefold-repetition/insufficient-material/fifty-move draws. `is_check` is dropped from the schema outright, not kept dormant — the concept it represented no longer exists, and a column that's always `false` going forward would actively mislead a future reader more than its absence would.

Technically, this requires move legality that chess.js's public API refuses to produce: `.move()`/`.moves()` always filter out anything that would leave the mover's own king in check, and — as a consequence of that filter making such positions unreachable through normal play — never has occasion to generate a move onto an enemy king's square either. `src/lib/game/pseudo-legal-moves.ts` (and its hand-kept-in-lockstep Deno copy in `supabase/functions/submit-move/index.ts`, same cross-runtime-boundary constraint as `redact_fen`/`redact-fen.ts`) uses chess.js's own private `_moves({legal: false})`/`_makeMove`/`_moveToSan`/`_incPositionCount` internals instead — undocumented but real, verified against the installed version via a dedicated spike before use, with a permanent canary test that fails loudly if a future chess.js upgrade silently changes them. We rejected the alternative of temporarily removing the mover's own king from the board to disable chess.js's public check-safety filter (a common trick for "no-check" variants elsewhere): `_moves()` skips empty origin squares, so that approach silently drops the king's own moves from the candidate list along with the veto, which is a worse and subtler bug than relying on documented-adjacent-but-private internals with a canary test backstopping them.

We rejected keeping checkmate/stalemate detection active server-side while simply not displaying it to players — that would leave the server enforcing a rule ("you cannot leave your own king in check") that's supposed to no longer exist, producing move rejections a player has no way to understand from what they can see, which is exactly the silent-information-mismatch problem this ADR exists to avoid.
